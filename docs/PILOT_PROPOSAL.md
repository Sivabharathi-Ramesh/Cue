## 1. Summary

Cue is a phone-only reading and recognition assistant for blind and low-vision users. It reads printed text aloud, names everyday objects with colour and direction, and answers simple questions such as the time, date, location, and battery level. This pilot is **English-first**. Hindi support exists in the build but is outside this pilot and will be validated separately. Reading and object recognition run on the phone without internet.

We are asking [NGO / club] to host a **12-week pilot with 20–30 blind and low-vision adults**. The pilot will measure whether Cue helps participants complete everyday tasks independently, and whether they keep using it after training.

## 2. The need

- India has one of the largest blind and low-vision populations in the world.
- Many people in this group own an Android phone, but mainstream apps assume the user can see a menu, tap small controls, and often stay online.
- Tasks that sighted people do without thinking — reading a medicine label, checking a bill, finding a dropped key — still require asking for help.
- Existing free apps are excellent but are often English-first, menu-driven, and designed for users comfortable with a screen reader and a data connection.

Cue is designed for the person who cannot see the screen at all: one live camera, one physical key, English-first, and no menu.

## 3. The solution

- **Live reading:** point the camera at printed text; Cue speaks it sentence by sentence, with pause, repeat, next, previous, and progress.
- **Object mode:** names up to three objects, with colour, left/ahead/right direction, and a coarse distance.
- **Direct questions:** “what is on my left?”, “what colour is this?”, “what time is it?”, “where am I?”, “battery?”
- **Honest failure:** if the text is handwritten or unreadable, Cue says it cannot read it rather than guessing.
- **Offline and private:** no account, no upload, no images or audio stored.

**Scope limitation:** this pilot covers **printed text only**. Handwriting is not supported. Cue will say it cannot read handwritten marks rather than guess. Handwriting is a Phase 2 evaluation; it may require a different model or an optional online mode.

## 4. Pilot objectives

1. Measure whether participants can complete five everyday tasks unaided after training.
2. Measure whether they continue to use Cue weekly after the training period.
3. Identify the tasks, devices, and environments where Cue fails.
4. Produce a training guide and an evidence report that can support a larger project.

## 5. Pilot design

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Participants | 20–30 blind and low-vision adults, ages 16–60 |
| Language scope | English only. Hindi remains in the build but is validated in a separate phase. |
| Sites | Two sites: a blind school/association and a low-vision clinic or community centre |
| Devices | Participants’ own Android phones where possible; 5 loaner phones if needed |
| Training | Two 90-minute sessions per site, plus a train-the-trainer session |
| Support | A named coordinator and a WhatsApp/phone helpline during the pilot |
| Duration | 12 weeks: 2 weeks setup, 8 weeks use, 2 weeks endline and analysis |
| Consent | Written/audio consent; participation voluntary; right to withdraw |

## 6. Measures and success criteria

| Measure | Method | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Unaided task success | Five-task checklist at baseline and endline | 70%+ after training |
| Weekly use | Short weekly check-in plus opt-in usage counts | 50%+ active at week 8 |
| Would recommend | Endline interview | 80%+ |
| Critical failures | Support log and interviews | Under 5% of tasks |
| Privacy incidents | Incident log | Zero |
| Time on task | Timed task checklist | Improve from baseline |
| Confidence | 5-point self-rating | Improve from baseline |

Secondary data: repeat requests, support tickets, device model, battery/heat issues, and the tasks users stop attempting.

## 10. Safeguarding and privacy

- No images or audio are stored by Cue; processing is on the phone.
- No accounts and no personal data are required to use the app.
- Pilot usage counts are opt-in, contain no personal data, and can be exported for the report.
- Written or audio consent; participants can withdraw at any time.
- The NGO’s safeguarding policy applies to all sessions, especially for participants under 18.
- A plain-language privacy note is provided to every participant.

## 11. Sustainability

- Cue remains free for blind and low-vision users.
- The pilot creates the evidence needed for a larger Rotary, CSR, or institutional project.
- After the pilot, support and training can be funded through institutional partnerships; the marginal cost of adding users is near zero.
- The code and training materials remain available to the implementing partner for the pilot.

## 12. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Device incompatibility | Test 3–5 phone models before recruitment; loaner phones for gaps |
| Voice recognition unavailable offline | Test offline packs; provide gesture fallback; evaluate offline command engine |
| Battery/heat during long sessions | Measure in setup; adaptive frame rate; charging guidance |
| Low uptake after training | Weekly check-ins; WhatsApp support; refresher session at week 4 |
| Overclaiming accuracy | Publish failures; train users on when not to trust the app |
| Handwriting expectations | State printed-only scope before recruitment; use printed materials in training; record handwriting as out of scope |
| Privacy concerns | On-device processing; no accounts; plain-language consent |
| Staff turnover | Train two trainers per site; written guide and videos |
