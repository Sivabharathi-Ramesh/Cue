# Cue

### A phone that reads and recognises for people who cannot see the screen.

**Cue is a working Android prototype for blind and low-vision users.** One live camera. No menu. One physical button. English-first for this pilot. Reading and object recognition run on the phone, without internet.

---

## The problem

- Millions of people in India live with blindness or low vision.
- Most have a smartphone, but most apps assume the user can see a menu and tap small buttons.
- Everyday tasks — a medicine label, a bill, a dropped key, the time — still require asking someone else.

## What Cue does

- **Reads printed text aloud** — letters, bills, labels, pages — in English or Hindi.
- **Names objects** — “blue mug, ahead, about one metre” — with colour, direction, and rough distance.
- **Answers direct questions** — “what is on my left?”, “what colour is this?”, “what time is it?”, “where am I?”
- **Works offline for reading and recognition** — no account, no upload, no internet.
- **Refuses honestly** — if it cannot read the marks, it says so instead of guessing.

## Why Cue is different

- **One camera, no mode menu.** The phone decides whether it is looking at text or an object.
- **One physical key.** Hold volume-down, speak, release. Easier than finding a button on a touchscreen.
- **English-first.** We are validating one language properly before adding another. Hindi is in the build and will be validated separately.
- **Built for a budget Android phone**, not a flagship or a headset.
- **Privacy by design.** No accounts. No images or audio stored. No data sold.

## What we have built

- 293 automated tests passing.
- A working Android release build with on-device OCR and object detection.
- English voice commands, help, reading progress, time, date, location, battery, and torch control.
- A design that prefers an honest refusal over a dangerous guess.

## The pilot we are asking for

- **20–30 blind and low-vision adults**, two sites, 12 weeks.
- Two training sessions, a coordinator, and WhatsApp support.
- Tasks: read a medicine label, read a bill or letter, identify three objects, tell the time, find a dropped object.
- Success measures: unaided task success, weekly use, confidence, support tickets, and zero privacy incidents.
- A written baseline and endline report, three case studies, and a training guide.

## What we need from you

- Access to your low-vision, blind-school, or association network.
- One coordinator and a training venue.
- **₹1.5–3 lakh or in-kind support** for training, transport, and evaluation.
- Introductions to your eye-health and rehabilitation partners.

**We are not asking for a national rollout. We are asking for a pilot that can prove whether this works in daily life.**
